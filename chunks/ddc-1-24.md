## la

{¶1} Hactenus regenerati hominis auctio ad Patrem solum relata: sequitur relata ad Patrem et Filium.

{¶2} Relata ad Patrem et Filium est **UNIO** et **COMMUNIO** cum Patre in Christo Filio, et ad imaginem Christi glorificatio.

{¶3} Unio ista et communio docetur Joan. xiv. 20. *in illo die vos cognoscetis me esse in Patre meo, et vos in me, et me in vobis.* et v. 23. *si quis diligit me, sermonem meum servabit, et Pater meus diliget eum, et ad eum veniemus, et apud eum habitabimus.* et cap. xvii. 21, 22, 23. *ut omnes unum sint, sicut tu Pater in me, et ego in te, ut et ipsi in nobis unum sint—. et ego gloriam quam dedisti mihi, dedi iis; ut sint unum, sicut et nos unum sumus, ego in iis, et tu in me, ut sint consummati in unum.* 1 Cor. vi. 17. *qui agglutinatur Domino, unus cum eo Spiritus est.* 1 Joan. ii. 23. *quisquis negat Filium, nec Patrem habet: qui profitetur Filium, etiam Patrem habet.* et iii. 24. *qui observat ejus præcepta, in eo habitat, et ipse in eo: et per hoc novimus eum habitare in nobis, ex Spiritu quem dedit nobis.* et cap. i. 3, 6, 7. *et communio nostra sit cum Patre et cum Filio ejus Jesu Christo: si dixerimus nos communionem habere cum eo, et in tenebris <!-- p.280 -->ambulamus, mentimur, nec sincere agimus: quod si in luce ambulamus, sicut ipse est in luce, communionem habemus cum eo mutuam.* et iv. 13, 15, 16. *per hoc cognoscimus nos in eo habitare, et ipsum in nobis, quod de Spiritu suo dedit nobis: quisquis professus fuerit Jesum esse Filium Dei, Deus in eo habitat, et ipse in Deo: et nos cognovimus et credidimus charitatem quam habet Deus erga nos. Deus charitas est; et qui manet in charitate, in Deo manet, et Deus in eo.*

{¶4} **COMMUNIO** quæ ex ista unione oritur est omnium donorum ac meritorum Christi participatio per Spiritum. Joan. vi. 56. *qui edit carnem meam et bibit meum sanguinem, in me manet, et ego in eo.* Rom. viii. 9. *si quis Spiritum Christi non habet, is non est ejus.* et v. 32. *quomodo non etiam cum eo nobis omnia donabit?* 1 Cor. i. 9. *fidelis est Deus, per quem vocati estis in communionem Filii ipsius Jesu Christi Domini nostri.* Eph. iii. 17. *inhabitet Christus per fidem in cordibus vestris.* Apoc. iii. 20. *si quis audierit vocem meam, et aperuerit ostium, ingrediar ad eum, et cœnabo cum eo, et ipse mecum.* 2 Cor. xiii. 13. *communicatio Spiritus.*

{¶5} Ex hac nostra communione cum Christo oritur mutua membrorum ejus communio inter se; quæ **SANCTORUM COMMUNIO** in symbolo Apostolico dicitur. Rom. xii. 4, 5. *quemadmodum enim in uno corpore membra multa habemus, membra vero omnia eandem non habent actionem; ita multi illi unum corpus sumus in Christo, singulatim autem alii aliorum membra.* 1 Cor. xii. 12, 13. *sicut corpus unum est, et membra habet multa, omnia vero illa membra corporis quod unicum est multa sunt, sed unum est corpus; ita et Christus: etenim per unum Spiritum nos omnes in unum corpus baptizati sumus, et Judæi, et Græci, et servi, et liberi: et omnes poti sumus in unum Spiritum.* et v. 27. *vos estis corpus Christi, et membra particulatim.*

{¶6} Ex hac unione et communione cum Patre et Christo, membrorumque Christi inter se, nascitur illud corpus mysticum quæ **ECCLESIA INVISIBILIS** est; cujus caput est Christus. 1 Thess. i. 1. *ecclesiæ Thessalonicensium, quæ est in Deo Patre, et Domino Jesu Christo.* et 2 Thess. i. 1. <!-- p.281 -->idem. Joan. xi. 52. *nec tantum pro ea gente, sed ut etiam filios Dei dispersos congregaret in unum.* 2 Cor. vi. 16. *templum estis Dei.* Gal. iv. 26. *illa vero quæ sursum est Hierusalem, libera est, quæ est mater omnium nostrum.* Eph. i. 22, 23. *eumque constituit caput super omnia ipsi ecclesiæ; quæ est corpus ipsius, et complementum ejus qui omnia implet in omnibus.* et iv. 13, 15, 16. *donec deveniamus nos omnes in unitatem fidei et agnitionem Filii Dei, in virum adultum, ad mensuram plenæ staturæ Christi—: prorsus adolescamus in eum qui est caput, Christus, ex quo totum corpus congruenter coagmentatum et compactum per omnes suppeditatas commissuras, ex vi intus agente pro mensura uniuscujusque membri, incrementum capit corpori conveniens, ad sui ipsius ædificationem per charitatem.* et v. 23. *Christus est caput ecclesiæ, et is est qui salutem dat corpori.* Col. i. 18, 19. *estque caput corporis ecclesiæ.* et ii. 19. *neque retinens caput, ex quo totum corpus per commissuras et connexus suppeditatum et compactum, augescit Dei augmento.* et i. 24. *pro corpore ipsius, quod est ecclesia.* Heb. iii. 6. *Christus ut Filius domui suæ præest: cujus domus sumus nos—.* et xii. 22, 23. *accessistis ad montem Sion et civitatem Dei vivi, Hierosolymam cœlestem, et myriadas angelorum, conventum universalem et concionem primogenitorum qui conscripti sunt in cœlis, et judicem universorum Deum, et spiritus justorum consummatorum.*

{¶7} Cum itaque corpus Christi unum mystice sit, communionem quoque membrorum ejus necesse est esse mysticam, et non necessario localem, tot nempe gentium longe dissitarum omniumque ab orbe condito ætatum. Rom. ii. 29. *sed qui in occulto est, Judæus est, et circumcisio cordis, in Spiritu, non litera, cujus laus non ex hominibus est, sed ex Deo.* Eph. ii. a v. 19. usque ad finem capitis. *non amplius estis peregrini et inquilini, sed concives sanctorum, ac domestici Dei: superstructi super fundamentum apostolorum ac prophetarum, existente imo angulari ejus lapide Jesu Christo, in quo totum ædificium congruenter coagmentatum, crescit ut sit templum sanctum Domino: in quo et vos una ædificamini ut sitis domicilium Dei per Spiritum.* Col. ii. 5. *etsi enim corpore absum, Spiritu tamen sum vobiscum, gaudens, et cernens vestrum ordinem et soliditatem vestræ in Christum fidei.*

{¶8} Amor Christi in hanc suam ecclesiam invisibilem et immaculatam illustratur simili amore conjugali. Apoc. xix. 7. *venerunt nuptiæ agni, et uxor ejus paravit se.*

{¶9} Tuendæ ac docendæ ecclesiæ ratione Christus *pastor* etiam dicitur. Joan. x. 14. *ego sum pastor ille bonus.* et v. 16. *fiet unus grex et unus pastor.* Heb. xiii. 20. *Deus pacis magnum illum ovium per sanguinem pacti æterni pastorem Dominum nostrum Jesum Christum ex mortuis reduxit.* 1 Pet. v. 4. *pastorum princeps.*

## en-sumner

{¶1} Hitherto the increase of the regenerate has been considered in its relation to the Father alone. We are now to consider that increase which has reference to the Father and Son conjointly.

{¶2} This consists in our **UNION** and **FELLOWSHIP** with the Father through Christ the Son, and our glorification after the image of Christ.

{¶3} Of this union and fellowship mention is made John xiv. 20. *at that day ye shall know that I am in my Father, and ye in me, and I in you.* v. 23. *if a man love me, he will keep my words, and my Father will love him, and we will come unto him, and make our abode with him.* xvii. 21—23. *that they all may be one, as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us......and the glory which thou gavest me I have given them, that they may be one, even as we are one; I in them, and thou in me, that they may be made perfect in one.* 1 Cor. vi. 17. *he that is joined to the Lord, is one spirit.* 1 John ii. 23. *whosoever denieth the Son, the same hath not the Father; but he that acknowledgeth the Son, hath the Father also.* iii. 24. *he that keep<!-- p.383 -->eth his commandments dwelleth in him, and he in him: and hereby we know that he abideth in us, by the Spirit which he hath given us.* i. 3, 6, 7. *truly our fellowship is with the Father, and with his Son Jesus Christ: if we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in darkness, we lie, and do not the truth; but if we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship one with another.* iv. 13, 15, 16. *hereby know we that we dwell in him, and he in us, because he hath given us of his Spirit: whosoever shall confess that Jesus is the Son of God, God dwelleth in him, and he in God: and we have known and believed the love that God hath to us: God is love, and he that dwelleth in love dwelleth in God, and God in him.*

{¶4} **THE FELLOWSHIP** arising from this union consists in a participation, through the Spirit, of the various gifts and merits of Christ. John vi. 56. *he that eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood, dwelleth in me, and I in him.* Rom. viii. 9. *if any man have not the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his.* v. 32. *how shall he not with him also freely give us all things?* 1 Cor. i. 9. *God is faithful, by whom ye were called unto the fellowship of his Son Jesus Christ our Lord.* Eph. iii. 17. *that Christ may dwell in your hearts by faith.* Rev. iii. 20. *if any man hear my voice, and open the door, I will come in to him, and sup with him, and he with me.* 2 Cor. xiii. 14. *the communion of the Holy Ghost.*

{¶5} From this our fellowship with Christ arises the mutual fellowship of the members of Christ's body among themselves, called in the Apostles' Creed **THE COMMUNION OF SAINTS.** Rom. xii. 4, 5. *for as we have many members in one body, and all members have not the same office; so we, being many, are one body in Christ, and every one members one of another.* 1 Cor. xii. 12, 13. *as the body is one, and hath many members, and all the members of that one body, being many, are one body, so also is Christ: for by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body, whether we be Jews or Gentiles, whether we be bond or free; and have been all made to drink into one Spirit.* v. 27. *ye are the body of Christ, and members in particular.*

<!-- p.384 -->

{¶6} Lastly, from this union and fellowship of the regenerate with the Father and Christ, and of the members of Christ's body among themselves, results the mystical body called **THE INVISIBLE CHURCH**, whereof Christ is the head. 1 Thess. i. 1. *unto the church of the Thessalonians which is in God the Father, and in the Lord Jesus Christ.* See also 2 Thess. i. 1. John xi. 52. *not for that nation only, but that also he should gather together in one the children of God that were scattered abroad.* 2 Cor. vi. 16. *ye are the temple of the living God.* Gal. iv. 26. *Jerusalem which is above is free, which is the mother of us all.* Eph. i. 22, 23. *he gave him to be the head over all things to the church, which is his body, the fulness of him that filleth all in all.* iv. 13, 15, 16. *till we all come in the unity of the faith, and of the knowledge of the Son of God, unto a perfect man, unto the measure of the stature of the fulness of Christ; that we may grow up into him in all things, which is the head, even Christ; from whom the whole body fitly joined together and compacted by that which every joint supplieth, according to the effectual working in the measure of every part, maketh increase of the body unto the edifying of itself in love.* v. 23. *Christ is the head of the church, and he is the Saviour of the body.* Col. i. 18, 19. *he is the head of the body, the church.* ii. 19. *not holding the head, from which all the body by joints and bands having nourishment ministered, and knit together, increaseth with the increase of God.* i. 24. *for his body's sake, which is the church.* Heb. iii. 6. *Christ as a son over his own house, whose house are we.* xii. 22, 23. *ye are come unto Mount Sion, and unto the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to an innumerable company of angels, to the general assembly and church of the first-born, which are written in heaven, and to God the Judge of all, and to the spirits of just men made perfect.*

{¶7} Seeing then that the body of Christ is mystically one, it follows that the fellowship of his members must also be mystical, and not confined to place or time, inasmuch as it is composed of individuals of widely separated countries, and of all ages from the foundation of the world. Rom. ii. 29. *he is a Jew which is one inwardly, and circumcision is that <!-- p.385 -->of the heart, in the spirit, and not in the letter; whose praise is not of men, but of God.* Eph. ii. 19—22. *now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellow citizens with the saints, and of the household of God; and are built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner-stone; in whom all the building fitly framed together, groweth unto an holy temple in the Lord: in whom ye also are builded together for an habitation of God through the Spirit.* Col. ii. 5. *though I be absent in the flesh, yet am I with you in the spirit, joying and beholding your order, and the stedfastness of your faith in Christ.*

{¶8} The love of Christ towards his invisible and spotless Church is described by the appropriate figure of conjugal love.[^s6] Rev. xix. 7. *the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready.*

{¶9} Christ is also called *the Shepherd,* by reason of his protecting and teaching the church. John x. 14. *I am the good shepherd.* v. 16. *there shall be one fold, and one shepherd.* Heb. xiii. 20. *now the God of peace, that brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, that great shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant—.* 1 Pet. v. 4. *when the chief shepherd shall appear—.*

## headnote

**Place.** The regenerate man's increase has so far been considered in relation to the Father alone (I.xxii–I.xxiii). Here it has reference to the Father and the Son together: union and fellowship with the Father in Christ, and glorification after Christ's image. Glorification follows in I.xxv.

**Argument.** Union and fellowship are shown from John and 1 John. Fellowship is a participation, through the Spirit, in all Christ's gifts and merits. From it comes the members' fellowship with one another, the creed's communion of saints. From union and fellowship together comes the mystical body, the invisible church, whose head is Christ. Because that body is one mystically, the fellowship of its members is mystical and not necessarily local: it spans distant nations and every age since the creation. Christ's love for this church is figured as married love, and he is called its shepherd because he protects and teaches it.

**Pressure point.** Milton defines the church first as invisible and mystical, before any visible church (I.xxix), and sets its fellowship outside any one place. Sumner's single note glosses the marriage figure from two of Milton's own divorce tracts.
